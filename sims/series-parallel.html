Old string lights had one famous failure mode: a single dead bulb took the whole strand with it. Modern ones don't, and the reason is a tiny wire most people have never noticed. Kill a bulb in each strand below and watch what the wiring decides to do about it.

Simplified: assumes an ideal voltage source (no internal resistance) and identical bulb resistances within each topology — real series-strand bulbs and parallel-strand bulbs are actually rated for different voltages by design, which this sim doesn't model. Filament resistance also rises with temperature, so a real survivor brightens slightly less than the formula predicts. The shunt mechanism described here is how real miniature string lights are built — look for the small ceramic-coated wire wrapped around the base of a spare bulb sometime.
