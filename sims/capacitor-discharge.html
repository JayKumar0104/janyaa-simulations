Press Plug in. The bulk capacitor inside a PC power supply charges to roughly 400 V DC from the wall — that's the number a technician has to respect.

Simplified: real bulk capacitors, bleed resistors, and safety standards vary by supply — 400 V, 470 µF, and the 50 V threshold are realistic round numbers for teaching, not a spec for any specific PSU. The discharge clock here runs 15× real time so the lesson fits a workshop; real PSU capacitors can take minutes to tens of minutes to bleed down, which is exactly why the label says "no user-serviceable parts inside." Never open a power supply, even unplugged — this sim is why.
