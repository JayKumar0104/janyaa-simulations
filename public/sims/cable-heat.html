A cable that fits is not the same as a cable that delivers. Every centimetre of thin copper is a resistor in the path, and the voltage it eats never reaches the phone. The missing volts don't vanish — they come out of the cable as heat.

Simplified: models the cable as a plain resistor carrying a fixed current and ignores connector resistance and the charger's own regulation response. The ×2 length accounts for the round trip — current flows out on one conductor and back on the other, and both halves add resistance. The 4.5 V pass/fail line is a rounded teaching threshold, not a spec from any real fast-charge protocol.
