Both squares below are the same piece of silicon — same cost, same space. The difference is what the space was spent on. Look at how much of each chip is actually doing math, then find out what the GPU gave up to get there.

The trade: a math unit needs a control unit to tell it what to do. A CPU gives every math unit its own. A GPU makes 16 share one — so all 16 must do the exact same thing at the same instant.

Why this is the whole story. A GPU didn't get 32× more math units by being better engineered — it got them by refusing to buy 128 separate control units and 128 separate caches. That purchase decision is permanent and it's why a GPU is spectacular at one kind of work and poor at the other. Training a neural network is millions of copies of the same tiny formula, so every lane stays busy. Simplified: real GPUs group 32 lanes per control unit rather than 16, and the area percentages here are rounded teaching figures.
